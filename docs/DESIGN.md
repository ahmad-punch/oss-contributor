# Design

The main decision is that the plugin advises and guards but never acts for the author in public: skills stop at a checklist, a failing test, or reply notes, and nothing posts a PR, comment, or review reply; the one enforcing piece, the commit trailer guard, is opt-in per repository (`.no-ai-trailers`) or per user (`OSS_NO_AI_TRAILERS=1`). Projects disagree on AI contributions (Kubernetes forbids AI trailers, the Linux kernel and Mesa require `Assisted-by:`, others ban AI code), so a global block would be wrong somewhere, and maintainers increasingly judge the person behind a PR by words they expect that person to have written. The trade-off is friction: the author does the last step by hand every time, the guard protects nothing until it is switched on, and the SessionStart reminder relies on a keyword heuristic that can misread unusual policy wording, which is why it quotes the line it matched and `/pr-prep` reads the files in full before drafting anything.
